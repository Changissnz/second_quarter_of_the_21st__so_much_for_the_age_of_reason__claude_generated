# Second Quarter of the 21st: So Much for the Age of Reason

---

Numerous stories have already been written on the beginning of the second quarter of the 21st, coincidentally the fourth quarter of the rule of American President Crumbs. See [Changissnz/the_president_crumbs_era_in_one_nutshell__claude_generated](https://github.com/Changissnz/the_president_crumbs_era_in_one_nutshell__claude_generated) for an overview of the leading figures during the rule of Crumbs. What remains, and what those accounts only partially address, are a few major problems: the artificial intelligence economy, and the changing social conditions of the West.

Industries are the way of living for many people. There is just no way around this broad requirement of facilitating needs through the social fabric. The thing was, it was a widely held belief in commoner and underground life that a lot of the work — the kind not replaced by A.I. systems — was practically slave labor. That would be a heavy-handed term, but given the spotty wage security in developed nations such as the United States, its usage is not exactly ridiculous.

---

The artificial intelligence economy, out of the past 5000 years, was the greatest human effort in industrializing information utility. It emerged purely out of the excess of industrial-scale data and political reckoning. The first reason is obvious enough to understand: there are all of these details, the details occur in patterns and for certain subsets of the whole, and artificial intelligence could sort it all out and serve as an executive agent built on top of predictive capabilities. The second reason was met with a lot of protest. The degrees of discomfort in finance and living situation were direct cause for determining who would be the protestors. The developed world was trying to build a higher layer on top of the rest of the world. The initiative was especially called for because of the situation surrounding the Schlavian Civil War and the weakening leverage the West had over their own affairs. Population declines, infrastructural decay and political wars had done the job well enough for a significant percentage of the working age population to not have any official jobs to work anymore. And guess what? The official reason the corporatists were stating: A.I. was the reason.

It was during the fourth quarter of the rule of Crumbs that young and middle-aged adults began expressing significant concerns, not merely those of apocalyptic science fiction. The capital was a big question. The money seemed to go to the top. The stock market and private companies. It was also quite a global enterprise, and that aspect was hypothesized to be a leading factor in the ensuing distress brought to many regions of the world after President Crumbs stepped down from power.

Think. If there was a system in the United States to pay individuals, say, $70K a year for software development, and around the same for hardware-related work, there would have been a lot less people officially unemployed. This is to say the privateering commercial enterprise of the United States was not a straightforward matter. The big piece of wisdom? There is honor to be kept with a good product, and honor to be pawned with good business. And the United States, despite its having stayed snug and deep in the Arabian trap for close to three decades at that point in time, was having an awfully hard time treating Schinia and its vassal states like how it did to countries such as Kubia and parts of Hispania. One could not be honest and say that difference in treatment was due entirely to the most pure and wholehearted love in the whole entire universe. Schinia and its vassal states provided the West with rare earth metals, pharmaceutical ingredients, manufactured products, and just as importantly, peace. This American standard of foreign policy was quite practical, but also prone to faultiness in how it dealt with peoples on an ethnological basis. There was still business to be kept and grown in that time period, even with the effects of the American-Schinian trade wars. This story does not specify if there was a significant armed conflict between the West and the Schinian realm in the decades after Crumbs. But if there was, there would have been surprise from some of the participants and something more than just a metaphorical ounce of betrayal, to start the ball rolling.

The artificial intelligence economy, due to the technology's power of replication and enhancements, ate its own: its own data and also its own creators (figuratively, for the most part). So in terms of the face value of this sector of development during this time period, there would be one person saying that billions were just made and another weekly scientific breakthrough has been accomplished, all thanks to artificial intelligence. And all that may have been true, but the part about the money was, from the perspective of the general public, completely speculative. The money was not going to be used to pay any unemployed person a living salary. It might go fund the Schlavian Civil War or pay a tributary fee to Schinia's hardworking laborers at the hardware manufacturing factories. If the money was to be kept domestically, it would stay somewhere far off like a monument, a testament, to the greatness of industry's leaders. Capitalist or not, individuals would not disagree with that statement if they had nothing to lose.
